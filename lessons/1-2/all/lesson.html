Fired by AI, hired back: why the cheapest employee is the one you keep

In 2023, IBM’s chief executive said the company would pause hiring for back-office jobs and that AI could replace around 7,800 roles within five years. Three years later, IBM is doing the opposite: it is tripling its entry-level hiring in the US, including software developers. “Today’s entry-level employees are also tomorrow’s managers,” says its HR chief, Nickle LaMoreaux. If companies stop hiring juniors now, she warns, in a few years they will be poaching mid-level staff from rivals at a premium of around 30%.

IBM is not alone. In 2024, the Swedish fintech Klarna boasted that its AI assistant was doing the work of 700 customer service agents. A year later, its CEO admitted that cost had been too important in the decision, that quality had suffered — and Klarna started hiring people again. Australia’s biggest bank, Commonwealth Bank, let 45 call-centre staff go after launching a voice bot. When calls went up by 2,000 a week and team leaders had to answer the phones, the bank reversed its decision and admitted an “error”.

AI has become the number one reason that US companies give for layoffs: it was cited in more than 121,000 job cuts in 2026 by October, more than double the figure for the whole of 2025. But a survey by Orgvue found that 55% of leaders who made people redundant because of AI now say it was a mistake, and about a third of companies have already rehired staff. Gartner predicts that by 2027, half of the companies that cut customer service headcount because of AI will have rehired for similar roles. Critics call much of this ‘AI washing’: a cost-cutting exercise dressed up as innovation.

Why do the cuts cost so much? According to Gallup, replacing an employee costs between half and twice their annual salary — for a software engineer earning $120,000, that can mean $150,000. And when experienced people leave, institutional knowledge leaves with them: who the most loyal clients are, and why the old code still works. Orgvue estimates that companies spend about $1.27 for every $1 they save through cuts.

So what keeps people? Not free pizza. Young employees put learning first: for Gen Z, learning and development is one of the top three reasons for choosing an employer. They want diverse work instead of menial tasks — which AI really can take over — clear promotion paths, a healthy work-life balance and, at some firms, a paid sabbatical. Hopefully, by the end of the decade, managers will have learnt that AI should augment people, not replace them. The best way to keep good staff is to convince them that their employer will still be investing in them in five years’ time — and that they will be working alongside AI, not competing with it.

Based on: Fortune and Bloomberg (IBM, Feb 2026); Bloomberg (Klarna, May 2025); Bloomberg and Finextra (Commonwealth Bank, Aug 2025); Challenger, Gray & Christmas (Nov 2026); Orgvue; Gartner; Gallup; Deloitte Gen Z Survey 2025.

Terms from Exercise 1: AI can take over menial tasks; young people want diverse work, clear promotion paths, a healthy work-life balance and, at some firms, a paid sabbatical.
Other ideas: keep hiring juniors (IBM) so you have tomorrow’s managers; learning and development; don’t lose institutional knowledge; use AI to augment people, not replace them; show staff that the company will invest in them in the future.

Grammar Future Continuous and Future Perfect Simple

5A Look at the underlined verb forms in the article and match them with the definitions of the Future Continuous (1) and Future Perfect Simple (2).

1 We use the Future Continuous to talk about an event that will be taking place at a particular moment in the future.
now ────────── 〰〰✕〰〰 ────► future
Next Friday at 5 p.m. I’ll be presenting the release.
2 We use the Future Perfect Simple to talk about events in the future which will be finished by a certain time.
now ──── ● ───── ✕ ────► future
By next Friday I’ll have finished the release.
1. will be poaching
2. will have rehired
3. will have learnt
4. will still be investing
5. will be working

FC = Future Continuous (definition 1) · FP = Future Perfect Simple (definition 2)
